Transformers: A Short Overview

Transformers are a neural network architecture introduced in 2017 in the paper “Attention Is All You Need” by Google Brain. They replaced older sequence models like RNNs and LSTMs by relying entirely on an attention mechanism.

Core Idea: Self-Attention

Instead of processing words one by one, transformers look at the entire sentence at once.
Each token creates three vectors:

Query (Q)

Key (K)

Value (V)

Attention scores determine how strongly words relate to each other. This allows the model to capture long-range dependencies efficiently.

Architecture

A transformer layer contains:

Multi-head self-attention

Feed-forward neural network

Residual connections

Layer normalization

Models can be:

Encoder-only (e.g., BERT) for understanding tasks

Decoder-only (e.g., GPT) for text generation

Encoder-decoder (e.g., T5) for translation and transformation tasks

Why Transformers Matter

They enable:

Parallel training

Better long-distance context modeling

Large-scale language models

Most modern NLP systems today are built on transformer architectures.
